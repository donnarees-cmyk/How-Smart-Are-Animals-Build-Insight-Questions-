📘 OPEN EVIDENCE NOTEBOOK — read this if you need help
Villa the hero: Villa, a Newfoundland dog, rescued a young girl who was trapped in a snowdrift. This was an extraordinary act of help.
Clever Hans: Hans the horse appeared to solve number problems, but researchers learned that he responded to small cues in his trainer’s body language. He was not independently doing the math.
Better experiments: The Clever Hans double-blind investigation showed why scientists must guard against unintended hints and unsupported assumptions. Researchers developed more sophisticated tests.
Different abilities: Scientists may examine problem-solving and counting aptitude (the ability to understand quantities). The SOI, or Structure of Intellect, test looks at five factors of intelligence with many subcategories, not just one narrow skill.
Practice or reasoning? Trainability means learning and repeating a practiced behavior. True intelligence involves using reasoning to solve problems, especially unfamiliar ones.
People and animals: Understanding an animal’s thinking can change the tasks people ask it to do and how they interact with it. When reading gets confusing, reread carefully to repair understanding.
